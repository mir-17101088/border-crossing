The Alikadam Corridor

A documented Rohingya infiltration route — eleven waypoints from the zero-line on the Myanmar border down to Alikadam Bazar.

Scroll
Two Entry Points

Crossing the Zero-Line

Rohingya breach the Myanmar border on two informal footpaths near pillars 59 to 61. Both converge on the Mro village of Fatra Para — the first stop on Bangladeshi soil, where this route begins.

Border Village

Silence at Fatra Para

By 8:25pm on May 20, Fatra Para had fallen silent. The Mro village, tucked into the hills of Bandarban's Alikadam upazila less than a kilometre from the Myanmar border, had shut down for the night. On a hillside below, faint torch-beams emerged along the stream bed — three women in front, followed by a silent line of men.

The Descent

Descending to Jurum Jhiri

The journey begins before nightfall on the Myanmar side. Rohingya groups gather quietly on hilltops near border pillars 59 to 61. Once darkness thickens across the forest, they descend a steep ridge and cross the stream at Jurum Jhiri to enter Bangladesh.

Wet Transit

The Muddy Waters of Fatra Jhiri

Moving in absolute silence, it takes about an hour to reach Fatra Jhiri. This stretch is grueling. The stream is muddy and waist-deep in places, forcing travellers to remove their shoes and carry them, risking cuts from stones and roots.

Physical Exhaustion

Exhaustion at Pahar Bhanga

After wading through the water, the groups push towards Pahar Bhanga near the Matamuhuri River, roughly five kilometres from the border. By this point, travellers are completely soaked and exhausted.

Forest Cover

Safe Haven at Shindhu Mukh

In the early hours, they reach Shindhu Mukh, a remote riverside area. They wait here because they can watch for movement — if they sense anyone approaching, they vanish into the forest canopy where security forces rarely go at night.

The Roadway

Dori Para: The Paved Road

At first light, the journey resumes. Some change into dry clothes before heading towards Dori Para, where the paved road begins. From here, motorcycles pick them up in stages to begin the 40km trip towards Alikadam Bazar.

Surveillance Barrier

Poamuhuri Checkpoint Evasion

At the Poamuhuri BGB checkpoint, a motorbike carrying two Rohingya passengers slows but does not stop. Following a brief verbal declaration to a guard on duty, the bike is waved through without further screening.

Bypass Road

The Footpaths of Mendon Para

Near an army post at Mendon Para, passengers get off before the checkpoint. They take a narrow hill path running alongside the main road to evade detection. The motorcycle crosses the post completely empty and picks them up on the other side.

Security Gap

Krilai Para Surveillance Gap

The next barrier is the Krilai Para BGB post. In the field, a lone guard on duty — speaking on a phone — simply waves transport vehicles through without performing identity or security checks.

Urban Bypass

The Final Detour

Instead of approaching the final army checkpoint outside Alikadam Bazar via the main road, passengers exit the vehicles early to bypass the tracking cordon on foot.

Terminal Point

Arrival at Alikadam Bazar

Passengers cross through Nayapara and Mongcha Para before wading over the Matamuhuri River a final time. Once clear of the cordon, they disappear into Alikadam Bazar, taking up low-paid, unnegotiable labour in local markets, tea stalls, and construction sites.
